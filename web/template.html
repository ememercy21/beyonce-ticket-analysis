The Beyconomics of Ticket Buying

Across 55,813 resale sales at 29 stadium shows, the same seat sold for the most about a month before the show. Buyers who waited until show day paid 15–23% less for floor, lower, club and upper seats.

How this was made

Data

Resale sale records (time, section, row, quantity, price per ticket) for 29 North American stadium shows, February to October 2023. Seven of the 36 collected files were exact copies of other shows and were removed. There is no data for Philadelphia, St. Louis, Kansas City, LA Sept 2, Atlanta Aug 14 or Houston Sept 24.

Model

A generalized additive model of log price: seat tier, VIP package, row, quantity, weekend and multi-night features, a separate timing curve for each tier, a tour-wide calendar trend and an effect for each show. It explains 75% of the variation in price, with a median error of about $81 per ticket when predicting held-out sales.

Reading the curves

  • Each day closer to a show is also a calendar day later. Tour-wide swings, like the slump in early March, appear in every show's curve.
  • These are completed sales, not prices one buyer could have waited for. Seats still on sale near show day may be worse seats within a tier.

Caveats

  • Toronto and Vancouver prices may be in Canadian dollars.
  • VIP covers 348 sales across four packages. Its curves are much less certain than the other tiers'.
  • Seat tiers are mapped from each stadium's own zone names, so a "Lower" seat is comparable across venues but not identical.
Model and data: BeyTickets project, R scripts 01–03.
